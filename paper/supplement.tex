\section{Notation and Elementary Properties}

Let $\Gamma=(N,(A_i)_i,(u_i)_i)$ and $\Gamma'=(N,(A_i)_i,(v_i)_i)$ be compatible finite games.  Define the residual $r_i(a)=u_i(a)-v_i(a)$ and
\[
\devdist(\Gamma,\Gamma')
=
\max_{i,a_{-i},a_i,b_i}
\left|r_i(a_i,a_{-i})-r_i(b_i,a_{-i})\right|.
\]
For a fixed slice $(i,a_{-i})$, the maximum absolute pairwise difference equals the residual range:
\[
\max_{a_i,b_i}|r_i(a_i,a_{-i})-r_i(b_i,a_{-i})|
=
\max_{a_i}r_i(a_i,a_{-i})-\min_{a_i}r_i(a_i,a_{-i}).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Relabeling invariance]
For every compatible pair of games and every player--action relabeling $g$,
\[
\devdist(g\Gamma,g\Gamma')=\devdist(\Gamma,\Gamma').
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The relabeling bijectively permutes the tuples $(i,a_{-i},a_i,b_i)$ over which the maximum is taken and preserves the corresponding residual difference.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mixed extension]
If $\devdist(\Gamma,\Gamma')\le\delta$, then for every player $i$, opponent mixture $\sigma_{-i}$, and own mixed strategies $p_i,q_i$,
\begin{align*}
\Big|&[u_i(p_i,\sigma_{-i})-u_i(q_i,\sigma_{-i})]\\
&-[v_i(p_i,\sigma_{-i})-v_i(q_i,\sigma_{-i})]\Big|
\le\delta.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed pure opponent profile, all residuals lie in an interval of width at most $\delta$.  Two convex combinations of numbers in that interval differ by at most $\delta$.  Averaging over opponent profiles preserves the bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Exactness of the Projection LP}

Let $S$ generate the candidate finite group $G$.  The LP has surrogate payoff variables $\widehat u_i(a)$, residual bounds $L_{i,a_{-i}}$ and $U_{i,a_{-i}}$, and objective variable $t$:
\begin{align*}
\min\quad &t\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&L_{i,a_{-i}}\le u_i(a)-\widehat u_i(a)\le U_{i,a_{-i}}
&&\forall i,a,\\
&U_{i,a_{-i}}-L_{i,a_{-i}}\le t
&&\forall i,a_{-i},\\
&\widehat u_i(a)=\widehat u_{\pi_g(i)}(ga)
&&\forall g\in S,i,a,\\
&t\ge0.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Projection LP]
The LP optimum equals
\[
\delta_G(\Gamma)
=
\min_{\widehat\Gamma\in\Fix(G)}
\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an invariant surrogate.  The smallest interval containing the residuals in one slice has endpoints equal to their minimum and maximum, so its width is exactly that slice's contribution to $\devdist$.  The smallest feasible $t$ for the surrogate is therefore its strategic distance from $\Gamma$.  Conversely, every feasible solution has every residual range at most $t$.

Invariance under all generators implies invariance under every finite product of generators, hence under $G$.  Every game in $\Fix(G)$ satisfies the generator equalities.  Minimizing over the payoff variables therefore minimizes strategic distance over $\Fix(G)$.
\end{proof}

Let
$P=n\prod_i|A_i|$ and
$Q=\sum_i\prod_{j\ne i}|A_j|$.
The implementation uses $P+2Q+1$ variables, $2P+Q$ strategic inequalities, and at most $|S|P$ raw generator equalities before duplicate removal.  Optional zero-sum or common-payoff constraints are linear and do not alter polynomial solvability in the explicit table size.

\section{Cycle Characterization}

Let $V$ contain one vertex for each orbit of payoff coordinates $(i,a)$ under $G$.  Write $[i,a]\in V$ for the orbit of coordinate $(i,a)$.  For every player $i$, opponent profile $a_{-i}$, and ordered pair of distinct own actions $(b_i,a_i)$, insert an edge
\[
e:[i,(b_i,a_{-i})]\longrightarrow[i,(a_i,a_{-i})]
\]
with label
\[
c_e=u_i(a_i,a_{-i})-u_i(b_i,a_{-i}).
\]
The reverse ordered comparison is also present and has label $-c_e$ before orbit identification.  Parallel edges are permitted; for feasibility, parallel upper-bound edges with the same endpoints may be replaced by the smallest label.  Self-loops are retained.

An invariant surrogate assigns one potential $x_v$ to each vertex, with
$\widehat u_i(a)=x_{[i,a]}$.  Its error on comparison $e$ is
\[
\left|x_{\mathrm{head}(e)}-x_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}-c_e\right|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Maximum-mean-cycle formula]
Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of directed cycles, including self-loops, in the orbit graph.  With the convention that the maximum is zero when $\mathcal C$ is empty,
\[
\delta_G(\Gamma)
=
\max_{C\in\mathcal C}
\left(-\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}c_e\right).
\]
A critical cycle certifies that no smaller strategic error is possible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A potential has error at most $t$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
-t\le x_{\mathrm{head}(e)}-x_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}-c_e\le t
\label{eq:two-sided}
\end{equation}
for every edge.  The upper half is
\[
x_{\mathrm{head}(e)}-x_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}\le c_e+t.
\]
The lower half is the upper constraint for the reverse ordered comparison.  Thus the two-sided system is equivalent to directed difference constraints with lengths $c_e+t$.

A finite system $x_v-x_u\le\ell_{uv}$ is feasible if and only if its graph has no negative directed cycle.  Necessity follows by summing around a cycle.  For sufficiency, add a zero-length source edge to every vertex and use shortest-path distances; absence of negative cycles makes them finite and gives every constraint.

Hence $t$ is feasible exactly when
\[
0\le\sum_{e\in C}(c_e+t)
=
\sum_{e\in C}c_e+|C|t
\quad\forall C,
\]
or
\[
t\ge-\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{e\in C}c_e
\quad\forall C.
\]
Minimizing $t\ge0$ gives the formula, including the acyclic case.  At the optimum, shortest-path potentials construct a nearest invariant surrogate.
\end{proof}

Karp's dynamic program computes the value using weights $w_e=-c_e$ \cite{karp1978cycle}.  The implementation recovers a witness without assuming that a predecessor chain in Karp's value table is critical.  Let $\lambda$ be the maximum mean and set $w'_e=w_e-\lambda$.  The reduced graph has no positive cycle and, when it has edges, has a zero-weight critical cycle.  Max-plus path potentials satisfy
\[
h_{\mathrm{head}(e)}
\ge h_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}+w'_e.
\]
The associated slacks are nonnegative.  Around a zero-weight cycle they telescope and sum to zero, so every edge on that cycle is tight.  A directed-cycle search in the tight-edge subgraph returns a critical witness.  Separately, difference constraints with lengths $c_e+\lambda$ recover a nearest surrogate.  Tests cover empty graphs, self-loops, parallel-edge compression, player swaps, and randomized comparisons against the independent LP.

\section{Invariant Equilibrium Existence}

Let $X=\prod_i\Delta(A_i)$ be the mixed-profile space and
$X^G=\{\sigma:g\sigma=\sigma\ \forall g\in G\}$ its fixed set.  Averaging any profile over $G$ shows that $X^G$ is nonempty; it is also compact and convex.

For precision $\lambda>0$, define the logit response
\[
F_i^\lambda(\sigma)(a_i)
=
\frac{\exp(\lambda u_i(a_i,\sigma_{-i}))}
{\sum_{b_i\in A_i}\exp(\lambda u_i(b_i,\sigma_{-i}))}.
\]

\begin{lemma}
If $\Gamma$ is exactly $G$-invariant, then
$F^\lambda(g\sigma)=gF^\lambda(\sigma)$ for every $g\in G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Exact invariance maps the expected payoff of an action against $\sigma_{-i}$ to the expected payoff of the relabeled action against the relabeled opponent profile.  The softmax denominator is merely permuted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Invariant Nash equilibrium]
Every finite exactly $G$-invariant game has a Nash equilibrium in $X^G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equivariance implies that $F^\lambda$ maps $X^G$ into itself.  Brouwer gives a fixed point $\sigma^\lambda\in X^G$.  Compactness gives a convergent subsequence $\sigma^{\lambda_k}\to\sigma^*$ as $\lambda_k\to\infty$.

Suppose an action $a_i$ in the support of $\sigma_i^*$ is worse than $b_i$ against $\sigma_{-i}^*$ by margin $\gamma>0$.  By continuity, the margin is at least $\gamma/2$ for all large $k$.  At a logit fixed point,
\[
\frac{\sigma_i^{\lambda_k}(a_i)}
{\sigma_i^{\lambda_k}(b_i)}
\le e^{-\lambda_k\gamma/2}\to0.
\]
Since the denominator is at most one, the numerator tends to zero, contradicting positive limiting mass.  Every supported action is a best response, so $\sigma^*$ is an invariant Nash equilibrium.
\end{proof}

Combining this theorem with equilibrium transfer yields a $G$-respecting $\delta_G(\Gamma)$-Nash equilibrium in every finite game.

\section{Tightness of the Transfer Constant}

Fix $k\ge2$.  Player 1 has actions $1,\ldots,k$ and player 2 has two irrelevant actions.  Player 1's payoff is one exactly when action 1 is chosen and zero otherwise; player 2's payoff is zero.  Let $G_k$ cycle player 1's actions.

Every invariant surrogate gives player 1 equal payoff for all $k$ actions at each fixed action of player 2.  The original difference between action 1 and any other action is one, so strategic distance to every invariant surrogate is at least one.  The zero surrogate attains one, hence $\delta_{G_k}=1$.  Every invariant strategy of player 1 is uniform, with payoff $1/k$ and regret $1-1/k$.  Thus no universal transfer theorem can replace the additive coefficient one by a smaller constant.

\section{Group Averaging}

Define
\[
\mathcal P_G\Gamma=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\Gamma,
\qquad
\eta_G(\Gamma)=\max_{g\in G}\devdist(\Gamma,g\Gamma).
\]

\begin{theorem}
\[
\delta_G(\Gamma)
\le\devdist(\Gamma,\mathcal P_G\Gamma)
\le\eta_G(\Gamma)
\le2\delta_G(\Gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The average is invariant.  Convexity gives
\[
\devdist(\Gamma,\mathcal P_G\Gamma)
\le |G|^{-1}\sum_g\devdist(\Gamma,g\Gamma)
\le\eta_G(\Gamma).
\]
For any invariant $\widehat\Gamma$ and any $g$,
\begin{align*}
\devdist(\Gamma,g\Gamma)
&\le\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma)
+\devdist(\widehat\Gamma,g\Gamma)\\
&=\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma)
+\devdist(g\widehat\Gamma,g\Gamma)\\
&=2\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma).
\end{align*}
Minimize over invariant surrogates and maximize over $g$.
\end{proof}

The group average can be computed without enumerating the group: every payoff-coordinate orbit receives its arithmetic mean.  Generator-induced union--find therefore computes it in near-linear time in the enumerated generator edges.

\subsection{The Factor Two Is Tight in Zero-Sum Games}

Fix $d\ge2$.  The row player has actions $R=\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$ and the column player has actions
$C=\{0,1\}\times\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$.  Let $G_d$ cyclically permute all row actions and independently cyclically permute the column actions within each of the two blocks.  Define the row payoff, independent of the column's within-block index, by
\[
A_d(s,(c,t))
=
\begin{cases}
c, & s=0,\\
1-c, & s\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]
The column payoff is $-A_d$, so the game is zero-sum.

The zero game is an invariant zero-sum surrogate at strategic distance one: in every fixed column, the row-payoff range across row actions is one, and in every fixed row, the column player's payoff range across column actions is also one.  Conversely, every invariant surrogate gives all row actions the same payoff at a fixed column.  The distinguished row $s=0$ and any other row differ by one in the original game, so their deviation-incentive error is one.  Therefore
\[
\delta_{G_d}(\Gamma_d)
=
\delta_{G_d,\mathcal Z}(\Gamma_d)
=1.
\]

Under group averaging, the row-payoff mean in column block zero is $(d-1)/d$, while the mean in block one is $1/d$.  On the distinguished row, the residuals in the two blocks are
\[
-\frac{d-1}{d}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{d-1}{d}.
\]
Their range is $2-2/d$.  Every fixed-column residual range remains one, and every other row has residual range $2/d$, so
\[
\devdist(\Gamma_d,\mathcal P_{G_d}\Gamma_d)
=2-\frac{2}{d}.
\]
Thus the factor-two approximation is asymptotically tight even when both the original game and the nearest structured surrogate are zero-sum.

\section{Zero-Sum Programs}

Let $A$ be the row payoff matrix and $-A$ the column payoff.  For strategies $(x,y)$ define
\[
\Gap_A(x,y)=\max_i(Ay)_i-\min_j(x^\top A)_j.
\]
Writing $p=x^\top Ay$, the row regret is $\max_i(Ay)_i-p$, the column regret is $p-\min_j(x^\top A)_j$, and their sum is $\Gap_A(x,y)$.

The invariant-gap program is
\begin{align*}
\min_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}\quad &\alpha-\beta\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&Ay\le\alpha\mathbf1,\qquad x^\top A\ge\beta\mathbf1^\top,\\
&x\in\Delta_m,\qquad y\in\Delta_n,\\
&(x,y)\text{ respects }G.
\end{align*}
For fixed $(x,y)$, the smallest feasible $\alpha$ is $\max_i(Ay)_i$ and the largest feasible $\beta$ is $\min_j(x^\top A)_j$.  Hence the optimum is exactly the minimum saddle gap over invariant profiles.

If $A$ is exactly invariant and $y$ is invariant, row actions in the same orbit have identical expected payoffs; analogously for columns.  One best-response constraint per relevant action orbit is therefore sufficient.  Strategy probabilities are represented by one variable per global action orbit, with simplex equations weighted by orbit multiplicity.

For approximate zero-sum games, projection is constrained to the intersection of the fixed and zero-sum subspaces.  If the structured defect is $\delta_G^{\mathrm{zs}}$, an invariant equilibrium of the projected matrix has each original-player regret at most $\delta_G^{\mathrm{zs}}$ and total saddle gap at most $2\delta_G^{\mathrm{zs}}$.  The direct invariant-gap LP can only improve this a priori guarantee.

\section{Statistical Stability}

Let $S=\Fix(G)\cap\mathcal C$ for a closed linear class $\mathcal C$, and define
$d(\Gamma,S)=\inf_{H\in S}\devdist(\Gamma,H)$.

\begin{lemma}[Distance-to-set stability]
For compatible games $\Gamma$ and $\widetilde\Gamma$,
\[
|d(\Gamma,S)-d(\widetilde\Gamma,S)|
\le\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every $H\in S$,
\[
d(\Gamma,S)
\le\devdist(\Gamma,H)
\le\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma)+\devdist(\widetilde\Gamma,H).
\]
Take the infimum over $H$ and swap the two games for the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

Let $\widehat\Gamma$ be a nearest invariant surrogate of an estimate $\widetilde\Gamma$, with empirical defect $\widehat\delta$.  If $\sigma$ is an $\epsilon$-equilibrium of $\widehat\Gamma$, then
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_i^\Gamma(\sigma)
\le\epsilon+\widehat\delta+\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\]
If every payoff estimate has error at most $\xi$, then
$\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma)\le2\xi$.  For $P$ bounded payoff means, each estimated from $M$ independent observations in $[0,1]$, Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give
\[
\xi=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2P/\alpha)}{2M}}
\]
with probability at least $1-\alpha$ \cite{hoeffding1963probability}.  This proves the finite-sample bounds in the main paper.

\section{Additional Experimental Details}

The principal synthetic family begins with a quotient matrix and replaces every quotient action by interchangeable copies.  Perturbations include independent strategic noise, opponent-dependent nonstrategic shifts, structured heterogeneity, and Bernoulli payoff observations.

The critical-cycle audit checks that consecutive witness edges form a closed walk, the negative mean witness label equals the computed defect, and the reconstructed surrogate attains that defect.  Dedicated tests exercise the empty graph, critical self-loops, parallel-edge compression, and player-swapping symmetries, in addition to 100 randomized comparisons with the LP.

For the zero-sum Reynolds family, the experiment evaluates
$d\in\{2,3,4,5,8,12,20,40\}$.  It independently computes the structured LP defect, unrestricted cycle defect, and averaged-game distance and compares the ratio with $2-2/d$.

The role-assignment family is a common-payoff game in which players must occupy complementary roles.  Heterogeneous player-role preferences break exact interchangeability.  The family demonstrates why incentive and welfare preservation must be distinguished.

Runtime results use exactly lifted zero-sum games so that the full and quotient programs solve the same strategic problem.  Each timing is a median over repeated HiGHS solves after a warm-up.  Absolute times depend on hardware and solver version, so the experiment validates scaling rather than a universal wall-clock constant.
