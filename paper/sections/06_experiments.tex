\section{Experiments}

The experiments are deliberately small and diagnostic.  Their purpose is to test the theorem stack, compare sharp projection with inexpensive alternatives, and measure orbit compression rather than to claim large-scale learning performance.  All games are explicit, optimization uses SciPy/HiGHS, and every random-number generator has a fixed seed.  The full sweep uses seed 17, with 20 replicates at each perturbation scale, 40 replicates at each sample count, and repeated timing after solver warm-up.  Raw CSV files, figure scripts, and theorem-regression tests are included.

\paragraph{Strategic versus raw asymmetry.}
We first lift a small quotient matrix into a game with interchangeable action copies and add opponent-action-dependent payoff components of increasing magnitude.  These additions are nonstrategic by construction.  Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(a) shows the predicted separation: nearest-symmetric raw payoff error grows linearly, while strategic defect remains at numerical zero.  Payoff-level symmetry would therefore reject games with unchanged best-response structure.

\paragraph{Zero-sum certificate calibration.}
We add independent strategic perturbations to lifted zero-sum games, compute the zero-sum structured projection, solve the projected game, and evaluate the profile in the original matrix.  Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(b) compares actual saddle gaps with the certificate $2\zsdef$.  Every transferred profile satisfies its certificate up to solver tolerance.  The direct invariant-gap LP is often tighter because it optimizes the final profile rather than a worst-case transfer bound.

\paragraph{Averaging and sharpness.}
For the same perturbation family, we compare exact structured projection with orbit averaging.  Random perturbations remain well below the worst-case factor two.  Separately, we instantiate Proposition~\ref{prop:reynolds-tight} for $d\in\{2,3,4,5,8,12,20,40\}$.  The structured and unrestricted defects both equal one, while the measured averaging ratio matches $2-2/d$ and reaches $1.95$ at $d=40$.  Thus the asymptotic lower bound is not an artifact of leaving the zero-sum class.

\paragraph{Compression--fidelity frontier.}
We construct nested groups that progressively merge heterogeneous action copies.  Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(c) plots error against the number of action-orbit variables.  Enlarging the group reduces the representation from 48 variables to 6 while increasing the structured defect; the transferred saddle gap stays below its certificate at every point.  This operationalizes \eqref{eq:defect-monotone}: choose the most compressed group whose certified error is acceptable.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous role preferences.}
The two-agent common-payoff family in Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(d) contains nominally interchangeable agents with increasingly different role preferences.  Strategic projection continues to certify unilateral incentives, but invariant coordination can lose welfare.  This stress test illustrates that approximate symmetry controls exploitability, not equilibrium selection or social value.

\paragraph{General-sum correlated-equilibrium transfer.}
We repeat the perturbation experiment with independently generated payoffs for the two players.  For each game, we project onto the nearest invariant surrogate and compute an invariant welfare-maximizing CE in profile-orbit variables.  Figure~\ref{fig:correlated}(a) evaluates the transferred distribution in the original game.  Its maximum CE deviation gain remains below $\delta_G$, as predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:ce-transfer}.  We also optimize welfare directly over invariant $\delta_G$-CE of the original game; this remains certified while improving selection within the allowed incentive budget.

\paragraph{Correlation as a coordination resource.}
Figure~\ref{fig:correlated}(b) evaluates Proposition~\ref{prop:role-correlation}.  Independent invariant play succeeds with probability $n!/n^n$, which falls rapidly with the number of agents and roles.  The invariant CE assigns roles without collision and retains welfare one.  The CE LP independently recovers the analytic value for all explicitly enumerated instances through $n=5$.

\paragraph{Profile-orbit compression.}
For exactly symmetric general-sum games, we compare the standard welfare-maximizing CE LP with its invariant profile-orbit version.  Both achieve the same welfare, as Proposition~\ref{prop:orbit-ce} requires.  Figure~\ref{fig:correlated}(c) shows that duplicating each of three quotient actions increases the full joint-distribution representation to 1,296 variables, while the orbit program remains at nine.  The constraint count is unchanged in this implementation, so the result isolates savings from distribution-variable compression alone.

\paragraph{Orbit-reduced zero-sum computation.}
On exactly lifted matrix games, the ordinary equilibrium LP and orbit-reduced formulation both return numerical saddle gaps of zero.  As equivalent actions are duplicated, the full program scales with the original action count while the reduced program retains the quotient dimension.  Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-sampling}(a) shows the increasing separation through 512 actions per player; absolute timings are machine dependent, so we emphasize scaling rather than a universal speedup constant.

\paragraph{Finite-sample certificates.}
Each row-player payoff entry is treated as the mean of a Bernoulli variable and estimated from independent samples; the column payoff is its negative.  We project the empirical game, solve the surrogate, and evaluate against the known population matrix.  Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-sampling}(b) plots true maximum regret and the Hoeffding certificate from Corollary~\ref{cor:sampling}.  The certificate covers every trial and contracts at the expected $M^{-1/2}$ rate.  Its conservatism motivates variance-adaptive confidence sets.

\paragraph{Implementation checks.}
The test suite audits critical self-loops, parallel-edge compression, closed-walk witnesses, player-swapping symmetries, CE and CCE transfer, and invariant profile orbits.  Across 100 generated games, the critical-cycle value, witness mean, reconstructed-surrogate distance, and independent LP objective agree to numerical tolerance.  These diagnostics do not replace proof, but they guard against sign, orbit, and witness-extraction errors.
