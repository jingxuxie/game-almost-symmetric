\section{Fast Symmetrization and Compression}

The sharp projection requires an LP or cycle computation.  A closed-form alternative is the Reynolds average
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_G\Gamma
=
|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\Gamma,
\label{eq:reynolds}
\end{equation}
computed without enumerating $G$ by averaging each payoff-coordinate orbit.

\begin{theorem}[Factor-two projection]
\label{thm:reynolds}
Let $\eta_G(\Gamma)=\max_{g\in G}\devdist(\Gamma,g\Gamma)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\delta_G(\Gamma)
\le
\devdist(\Gamma,\mathcal P_G\Gamma)
\le
\eta_G(\Gamma)
\le
2\delta_G(\Gamma).
\label{eq:reynolds-chain}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The first inequality is feasibility, the second follows by convexity, and the third applies the triangle inequality through any invariant surrogate.  Hence solving the averaged game loses at most twice the optimal strategic transfer error.  Averaging also preserves every relabeling-invariant convex linear class, including zero-sum and common-payoff games.

\begin{proposition}[Asymptotic tightness]
\label{prop:reynolds-tight}
For every $d\ge2$, a two-player zero-sum game and within-player group satisfy
\[
\delta_{G_d}(\Gamma_d)
=
\delta_{G_d}^{\mathrm{zs}}(\Gamma_d)
=1,
\qquad
\devdist(\Gamma_d,\mathcal P_{G_d}\Gamma_d)
=2-2/d.
\]
Thus factor two is asymptotically tight even relative to the nearest symmetric zero-sum surrogate.
\end{proposition}
The construction has one orbit of $d$ row actions and two column blocks: a distinguished row receives block payoffs $(0,1)$ and every other row receives $(1,0)$.  The zero surrogate attains distance one; invariance gives the matching lower bound, while orbit means $(d-1)/d$ and $1/d$ produce residual range $2-2/d$.  Full details are in the supplement.

For nested groups $G_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_K$, fixed subspaces shrink, so
\begin{equation}
\delta_{G_0}(\Gamma)
\le\cdots\le
\delta_{G_K}(\Gamma),
\label{eq:defect-monotone}
\end{equation}
while action and payoff-coordinate orbit counts decrease.  This gives a certified compression--fidelity frontier: choose the largest group within the desired incentive budget.
