\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The framework is most useful with a small hierarchy of semantically plausible groups.  Cheap generator discrepancies or Reynolds averaging can screen candidates; the sharp LP or cycle solver then returns the defect and a local obstruction witness.  With estimated payoffs, Theorem~\ref{thm:group-selection} replaces an informal choice from the compression frontier by a confidence-calibrated one: the selected group, merged coordinates, uncertainty radius, and final incentive budget are all auditable.  The no-$\log K$ observation applies because every candidate is evaluated from the same uniformly controlled payoff table; it would not automatically extend to groups proposed by an additional noisy discovery procedure without accounting for that procedure's error.

A symmetry-respecting Nash profile and a symmetry-respecting CE make different operational assumptions.  The former requires independent randomization; the latter requires a mediator, shared seed, dispatcher, or common signal.  Correlation can remove role collisions, but it should not be assumed when such coordination is unavailable.

The defect certifies unilateral incentives, not payoff levels, equilibrium selection, or welfare.  It also assumes a supplied group and an explicit normal-form table.  General-sum Nash computation remains hard after projection, whereas CE/CCE are tractable because their feasible regions are polyhedral.  Discovering approximate groups, exploiting compact graphical or sequential representations, and obtaining coverage-aware certificates from nonuniform or trajectory-generated data remain open directions.

\section{Conclusion}
We developed a quantitative theory of almost symmetric games: incentive-preserving projection, critical-cycle witnesses, equilibrium transfer, sharp group averaging, orbit-reduced Nash and correlated-equilibrium programs, and statistically certified selection from a symmetry hierarchy.  The results separate the fidelity of a symmetry prior from the coordination and data resources used to realize it, enabling explicit compression with checkable strategic guarantees.
