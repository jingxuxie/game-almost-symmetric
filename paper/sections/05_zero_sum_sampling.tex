\section{Zero-Sum and Statistical Certificates}

For a two-player zero-sum matrix $A$, let $\mathcal Z$ be the zero-sum subspace and define $\zsdef=\delta_{G,\mathcal Z}$.  For $x\in\Delta_m,y\in\Delta_n$, the saddle gap
\begin{equation}
\Gap_A(x,y)
=
\max_i(Ay)_i-\min_j(x^\top A)_j
\label{eq:saddle-gap}
\end{equation}
is the sum of the two player regrets.

\begin{corollary}[Transferred zero-sum certificate]
\label{cor:zs-transfer}
An equilibrium of a $G$-invariant zero-sum surrogate at distance $\delta$ has each original-player regret at most $\delta$ and saddle gap at most $2\delta$.  A nearest structured surrogate therefore gives gap at most $2\zsdef$.
\end{corollary}

A tighter a posteriori profile is obtained directly from
\begin{align}
\min_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}\quad
&\alpha-\beta
\label{eq:invariant-gap-lp}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&Ay\le\alpha\mathbf1,\nonumber\\
&x^\top A\ge\beta\mathbf1^\top,\nonumber\\
&x\in\Delta_m,\quad y\in\Delta_n,\nonumber\\
&(x,y)\text{ respects }G.\nonumber
\end{align}
For fixed $(x,y)$, the objective is exactly \eqref{eq:saddle-gap}; hence the optimum is the best invariant saddle gap and is at most $2\zsdef$.  In an exactly invariant game, one strategy variable and one best-response constraint per relevant action orbit suffice.

\paragraph{Estimated payoffs.}
Distance to a fixed closed linear class is one-Lipschitz:
\begin{align}
\big|\delta_{G,\mathcal C}(\Gamma)
&-\delta_{G,\mathcal C}(\widetilde\Gamma)\big|
\nonumber\\
&\le\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\label{eq:defect-stability}
\end{align}
If $\widehat\Gamma$ is the nearest invariant surrogate of an estimate $\widetilde\Gamma$ with empirical defect $\widehat\delta$, then
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_i^\Gamma(\sigma)
\le
\epsilon+\widehat\delta+
\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\]
If $P$ payoff means are each estimated from $M$ independent observations in an interval of length one, Hoeffding and a union bound imply, with probability $1-\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Reg}_i^\Gamma(\sigma)
\le
\widehat\delta
+2\sqrt{\frac{\log(2P/\alpha)}{2M}}.
\label{eq:sampling-certificate}
\end{equation}
The zero-sum saddle gap is at most twice this quantity.  Full proofs and variance-adaptive extensions are in the supplement.
