\section{Zero-Sum and Statistical Certificates}

The transfer theorem is equilibrium-agnostic, but two-player zero-sum games allow especially direct computation.  Let $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ be the row player's payoff matrix and $-A$ the column player's payoff.  Let $\mathcal Z$ denote the linear class of zero-sum games and define the structured defect
\[
\zsdef(\Gamma)=\delta_{G,\mathcal Z}(\Gamma).
\]
For strategies $x\in\Delta_m$ and $y\in\Delta_n$, define the saddle-point residual
\begin{align}
\Gap_A(x,y)
&=
\max_i (Ay)_i-\min_j(x^\top A)_j \nonumber\\
&=\operatorname{Reg}_{\rm row}(x,y)
+\operatorname{Reg}_{\rm col}(x,y).
\label{eq:saddle-gap}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[Transferred zero-sum certificate]
\label{cor:zs-transfer}
Let $\widehat\Gamma\in\Fix(G)\cap\mathcal Z$ satisfy
$\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma)\le\delta$, and let $(x,y)$ be a Nash equilibrium of $\widehat\Gamma$.  Then each player's regret in $\Gamma$ is at most $\delta$ and
\[
\Gap_A(x,y)\le2\delta.
\]
In particular, a nearest structured surrogate admits a $G$-respecting profile with saddle gap at most $2\zsdef(\Gamma)$.
\end{corollary}

One can also optimize the invariant profile directly in the original matrix game.  Consider
\begin{align}
\min_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}\quad &\alpha-\beta \label{eq:invariant-gap-lp}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&Ay\le\alpha\mathbf1,\qquad
x^\top A\ge\beta\mathbf1^\top,\nonumber\\
&x\in\Delta_m,\quad y\in\Delta_n,\quad (x,y)\text{ respects }G.\nonumber
\end{align}
The final condition consists only of linear equalities between probabilities in the same action orbit.

\begin{proposition}[Best invariant saddle gap]
\label{prop:direct-gap}
The optimum of \eqref{eq:invariant-gap-lp} equals
$\min_{(x,y)\text{ respects }G}\Gap_A(x,y)$.  It is zero for an exactly $G$-invariant zero-sum game and is at most $2\zsdef(\Gamma)$ in general.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $(x,y)$, the smallest feasible $\alpha$ is $\max_i(Ay)_i$ and the largest feasible $\beta$ is $\min_j(x^\top A)_j$, so the objective is exactly \eqref{eq:saddle-gap}.  Exact invariance gives an invariant equilibrium.  For an approximate game, apply Corollary~\ref{cor:zs-transfer} to an invariant equilibrium of a nearest structured surrogate.
\end{proof}

The LP can be expressed with one strategy variable per global action orbit.  If the matrix itself is exactly invariant, then $(Ay)_i$ is constant over every row-action orbit for invariant $y$, and $x^\top A$ is constant over every column-action orbit for invariant $x$.  Hence only one best-response constraint per orbit is needed.  This gives a quotient-size solver without changing the saddle gap.

\subsection{Payoffs Estimated from Samples}

Suppose the true game $\Gamma$ is unavailable and a payoff table $\widetilde\Gamma$ is estimated from data.  Distance to any fixed closed set is one-Lipschitz under a norm; the same statement holds for our quotient seminorm.

\begin{proposition}[Stability of the defect]
\label{prop:defect-stability}
For every relabeling-invariant closed linear class $\mathcal C$,
\[
\big|\delta_{G,\mathcal C}(\Gamma)
-
\delta_{G,\mathcal C}(\widetilde\Gamma)\big|
\le
\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\]
\end{proposition}

Let $\widehat\Gamma$ be a nearest $G$-invariant surrogate of the estimated game in class $\mathcal C$, and write
$\widehat\delta=\delta_{G,\mathcal C}(\widetilde\Gamma)$.  If $\sigma$ is an $\epsilon$-equilibrium of $\widehat\Gamma$, then the triangle inequality and Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} give
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Reg}_i^\Gamma(\sigma)
\le
\epsilon+\widehat\delta
+\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma).
\label{eq:stat-general}
\end{equation}
If every estimated payoff entry is within $\xi$ of its true mean, then \eqref{eq:raw-upper} yields
$\devdist(\Gamma,\widetilde\Gamma)\le2\xi$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-sample certificate]
\label{cor:sampling}
Assume each of the $P$ payoff means lies in $[0,1]$ and is estimated from at least $M$ independent bounded samples.  With probability at least $1-\alpha$,
\[
\xi_M
=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2P/\alpha)}{2M}}
\]
uniformly bounds all entrywise errors.  Therefore an exact equilibrium of the projected estimate has true per-player regret at most
\[
\widehat\delta+2\xi_M.
\]
In a two-player zero-sum game its true saddle gap is at most
$2\widehat\delta+4\xi_M$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Hoeffding's inequality to each payoff entry and take a union bound \cite{hoeffding1963probability}; then use \eqref{eq:stat-general}.  The zero-sum statement sums the two player regrets.
\end{proof}

The bound is distribution-free and intentionally conservative.  Tighter empirical-Bernstein, likelihood-region, or structure-aware confidence sets can replace the entrywise Hoeffding term without changing the projection-and-transfer argument.
