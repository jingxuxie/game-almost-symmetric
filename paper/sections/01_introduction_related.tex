\section{Introduction}

Symmetry can collapse a large multi-agent game into a small quotient and provide a principled convention when agents coordinate without a privileged labeling.  Exact symmetry is brittle, however: heterogeneous costs, estimated utilities, or small design perturbations can destroy every literal automorphism while leaving the strategic interaction nearly unchanged.

Raw payoff error is a poor remedy.  Adding an arbitrary function $c_i(a_{-i})$ to player $i$'s utility can move the payoff tensor arbitrarily far while preserving every unilateral incentive and equilibrium.  We instead study a game $\Gamma$ with a supplied relabeling group $G$ and ask for the smallest change in unilateral-deviation incentives that makes $G$ exact.  This \emph{strategic symmetry defect} $\delta_G(\Gamma)$ turns $G$ into a certified abstraction: project to an exactly invariant surrogate, solve in orbit variables, and transfer the solution back with an explicit incentive bound.

The framework separates fidelity, computation, and coordination.  A larger group gives more compression but may distort incentives; moreover, symmetry-respecting independent strategies and symmetry-respecting correlated distributions need not have comparable welfare. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an incentive-based symmetry defect, a sparse exact projection LP, and a maximum-mean-cycle characterization with a local lower-bound witness and constructive surrogate;
    \item additive $\epsilon+\delta$ transfer for Nash, correlated, and coarse-correlated equilibrium, including $G$-respecting $\delta_G$ solutions in every finite game;
    \item a profile-orbit LP for invariant correlated equilibria in general-sum games and a role-assignment separation: independent invariant play has welfare $n!/n^n$, whereas invariant correlation achieves one;
    \item a closed-form group average within factor two of optimal, an asymptotically tight zero-sum family, and orbit-restricted zero-sum exploitability programs; and
    \item laptop-scale validation of the certificates, sharp constants, compression--fidelity frontier, and value of correlation, together with finite-sample payoff guarantees.
\end{itemize}
