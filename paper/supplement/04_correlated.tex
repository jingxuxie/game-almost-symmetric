\section{Correlated and Coarse-Correlated Equilibrium}

For a joint distribution $\mu\in\Delta(A)$, define the unconditional CE deviation gain
\[
R^{\mathrm{CE}}_{i,a_i\to b_i}(\mu;\Gamma)
=
\sum_{a_{-i}}\mu(a_i,a_{-i})
[u_i(b_i,a_{-i})-u_i(a_i,a_{-i})].
\]
The distribution is an $\epsilon$-CE when all these gains are at most $\epsilon$.  For CCE, a player selects a fixed action before observing a recommendation:
\[
R^{\mathrm{CCE}}_{i\to b_i}(\mu;\Gamma)
=
\sum_a\mu(a)
[u_i(b_i,a_{-i})-u_i(a)]
\le\epsilon.
\]
A distribution respects $G$ when $\mu(ga)=\mu(a)$ for all $g,a$.

\begin{theorem}[CE and CCE transfer]
\label{thm:supp-ce-transfer}
If $\devdist(\Gamma,\widehat\Gamma)\le\delta$, every $\epsilon$-CE of $\widehat\Gamma$ is an $(\epsilon+\delta)$-CE of $\Gamma$.  The same holds for CCE.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed CE deviation $a_i\to b_i$, strategic distance bounds the pointwise difference between its gains in the two games by $\delta$.  Therefore
\begin{align*}
\big|R^{\mathrm{CE}}_{i,a_i\to b_i}(\mu;\Gamma)
&-R^{\mathrm{CE}}_{i,a_i\to b_i}(\mu;\widehat\Gamma)\big|\\
&\le
\delta\sum_{a_{-i}}\mu(a_i,a_{-i})
\le\delta.
\end{align*}
Adding the surrogate violation proves the CE claim.  For CCE, the same pointwise bound is averaged with weights summing to one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Invariant objective-optimal CE]
\label{thm:supp-invariant-ce}
Suppose $\widehat\Gamma$ is exactly $G$-invariant.  Let
$w:A\to\mathbb R$ satisfy $w(ga)=w(a)$.  Among the CE maximizing
$\sum_a\mu(a)w(a)$, some maximizer respects $G$.  The same statement holds for CCE.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $g\in G$, let $g\mu$ be the pushforward distribution.  Exact game invariance implies that $g$ bijectively relabels every CE incentive constraint.  Thus $g\mu$ is CE whenever $\mu$ is.  The CE polytope is convex, so
\[
\overline\mu=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\mu
\]
is feasible and invariant.  If $\mu$ maximizes the objective, every $g\mu$ has the same value because $w$ is invariant, and hence so does $\overline\mu$.  CCE constraints are relabeled in exactly the same way.
\end{proof}

Taking any linear objective, such as zero, proves existence of invariant CE and CCE.  Combining Theorems~\ref{thm:supp-ce-transfer} and
\ref{thm:supp-invariant-ce} with a nearest invariant surrogate yields a $G$-respecting $\delta_G$-CE and $\delta_G$-CCE in every finite game.

\subsection{Profile-Orbit Linear Program}

Let $\mathcal O_A$ be the pure-profile orbits under $G$.  Represent an invariant distribution by one nonnegative variable $z_O$ per orbit, interpreted as the probability of each individual profile in $O$.  The simplex constraint is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{O\in\mathcal O_A}|O|z_O=1.
\label{eq:supp-orbit-simplex}
\end{equation}
For CE, every ordered deviation produces the linear inequality
\[
\sum_{a_{-i}}
 z_{[(a_i,a_{-i})]}
[u_i(b_i,a_{-i})-u_i(a_i,a_{-i})]
\le\epsilon.
\]
There are $\sum_i|A_i|(|A_i|-1)$ such inequalities.  CCE instead has $\sum_i|A_i|$ fixed-deviation inequalities.  A linear objective $w$ contributes coefficient
$\sum_{a\in O}w(a)$ to variable $z_O$.

\begin{proposition}[Orbit LP exactness]
\label{prop:supp-orbit-ce}
For an explicit game, optimizing a linear objective over invariant CE or CCE is a polynomial-size linear program.  If the game and objective are exactly $G$-invariant, its optimum equals the unrestricted CE or CCE optimum.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first claim follows from the explicit constraint count above.  For the second, take an unrestricted objective-optimal equilibrium and average it over $G$ using Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-invariant-ce}.  The average is feasible, invariant, and objective-optimal, so it is represented in the orbit program.
\end{proof}

This gives a tractable end-to-end general-sum pipeline: compute a symmetric surrogate, optimize an invariant CE in profile-orbit variables, and certify its original-game incentive violation by Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-ce-transfer}.  The selection objective may be evaluated using the original estimated payoffs even though feasibility is imposed using the surrogate; transfer depends only on the surrogate incentive constraints.

\subsection{Independent and Correlated Role Assignment}

There are $n$ agents and $n$ roles.  Every agent receives common payoff one iff all selected roles are distinct, and zero otherwise.  The game is invariant to arbitrary player permutations and common role relabelings.

\begin{proposition}[Role-assignment separation]
\label{prop:supp-role}
The fully symmetry-respecting independent profile has welfare $n!/n^n$.  A fully symmetry-respecting CE has welfare one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Player and role symmetry force every marginal of an invariant product profile to be uniform over the $n$ roles.  Under independent uniform draws, all roles are distinct in exactly $n!$ of the $n^n$ joint outcomes.

For the correlated distribution, sample uniformly from the $n!$ permutations of the roles and privately recommend one role to each player.  The distribution is invariant to permuting agents and to relabeling roles.  Conditional on any recommendation, following it gives a distinct role and payoff one.  Deviating to any other role duplicates the role recommended to another player and gives payoff zero.  The distribution is therefore CE and has welfare one.
\end{proof}

Using Stirling's approximation, $n!/n^n\asymp\sqrt{2\pi n}e^{-n}$, whereas correlated welfare remains one.  This exponential separation demonstrates that welfare loss need not come from respecting symmetry; it can arise from the additional requirement that a symmetric solution factor into independent marginals.  In applications, the CE interpretation requires a mediator, shared random seed, dispatcher, or common signal.
